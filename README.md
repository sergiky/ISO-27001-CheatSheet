# ISO-27001-CheatSheet

We are working at RandomCorp

# Foundations

- ISO 27001 is a management system standard - how you decide and prove, not what you bought
- Clauses 4-10 are the requirements; Annex A is the control set you justify against your list
- Every concept lands on RandomCorp, so you see the decision and not just the definition

- ISO 27001 provides REQUIREMENTS for an ISMS - not guidelines on how to implement one
- 'Shall' is obligatory and auditable; 'should' is guidance. 27001 shalls, 27002 shoulds
- You certify against 27001 only - ISO 27002 certification does not exist

## Clauses 4-10 and Annex A
- Unnumbered introduction, then clauses 1-3 (scope, vocabulary), then clauses 4-10, then Annex A
- Clauses 4-10 are auditable and cannot be excluded - scope and control applicability are what your decided
- Annex A: 93 controls in 4 themes - Organizational 37, People 8, Physical 14, Technological 34


## PDCA
- Plan = clauses 4-7, Do = clause 8, Check = clause 9, Act = clause 10
- Check is the phase organizations skip because it produces bad news - finding your own NCs is success
- RandomCorp took 12 months, and the month-one scope decision shaped everything after it

# Context of the organization
- Internal issues are inside your boundary and changeable; external issues act on you and you respond
- SWOT and PESTLE are convention, not requirements - the clause cares that you determined the issues
- Documenting context is not required; determining monitoring and reviewing it is

Interested parties and their requirements
- An interested party can affect the ISMS or be affected by it - no commercial relationship needed
- The clause wants their requirements and which you'll address; satisfying everyone is not the goal
- Where a contract is stricter than the law, the contact is the number you must hit

# Scope statement line by line
- A good scope statement is short, specific and boring - named services, not 'our information systems'
- Every exclusion carries a justification, and every justification is a claim an auditor can verify
- Scope must stay consistent with the risk assessment, SoA, asset inventory and policy

# Leadership
- Clause 5.1 places duties on top management personally - several cannot be delegated to a security function
- Objectives must be compatible with strategic direction; ISMS requirements integrated into a real process
- The auditor's test: ask the CEO a question and see whether they answer or look at the CISO

## The information security policy 5.2
- The policy is not a staff rulebook it's a short, high-level statement of intent that top management own
- Two commitments must be explicit: satisfy applicable requirements, and continually improve the ISMS
- Documented, communicated internally, available to interested parties as appropriate - not the same as published.
 
## Roles, responsibilities and authorities
- Two responsibilities are named explicitly: ensuring conformance, and reporting performance to top management
- Clause 5.3 requires no document information - a RACI is convention, not obligation
- Controls belong with whoever runs the thing being controlled - that's integration in practice

# Planning and Risk
- Define criteria first - setting acceptance criteria after seeing results is how everything comes out acceptable
- The 2022 standards does not mandate an asset-based method; it demands consistent, valid, comparable results
- A risk owner need accountability and authority - if they can't approve the fix, they're not the owner

## Risk assessment
- A register with no spread of scores carries no information - the distribution is read first
- Acceptance threshold need real consequences attached, or they're just numbers in a spreadsheet
- Contractual and regulatory requirements can force treatment below your threshold
